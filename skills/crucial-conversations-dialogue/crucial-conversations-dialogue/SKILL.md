# 关键对话高风险沟通 Skill

## R - Source Grounding

This skill distills the book's core tools for high-stakes conversations:

- A crucial conversation has high stakes, opposing opinions, and strong emotions.
- Start with heart: clarify what you really want for yourself, the other person, and the relationship.
- Watch for safety problems: silence and violence signal that dialogue is failing.
- Restore safety through mutual purpose, mutual respect, apology, contrast, or CRIB.
- Separate facts, stories, and feelings.
- Use STATE to share your path; use AMPP to explore the other person's path.
- End with clear decisions, owners, deadlines, and follow-up.

## I - Interpretation

Treat conflict as a dialogue-safety problem before treating it as a persuasion problem. The agent should help the user slow down, clarify purpose, notice whether safety has collapsed, make it safe enough to continue, then express facts and interpretations tentatively while inviting the other side's view. The end state is not "winning"; it is shared meaning plus clear next actions.

## A1 - Application Examples

Book-backed examples include handling medical silence, workplace conflict, sensitive family disputes, and conversations where people retreat into silence or attack with violence. When making a script, mark invented facts as assumptions and keep the user's own words separable from recommended phrasing.

## A2 - Future Trigger

Use this skill when the user faces a conversation with high stakes, disagreement, and strong emotion, or asks for a script that preserves respect while addressing a difficult issue. Language signals include: "I need to confront", "hard conversation", "they shut down", "they got defensive", "how do I say this", "restore safety", "separate facts from stories", "STATE", "AMPP", "mutual purpose", and "action commitment".

Do not use it for ordinary status updates, debate coaching aimed at defeating someone, crisis intervention, active domestic violence, legal determinations, or HR investigations beyond suggesting appropriate escalation.

## E - Execution

1. Diagnose whether this is a crucial conversation.
   - Confirm stakes, disagreement, and emotion.
   - If any are absent, use lighter communication advice.

2. Start with heart.
   - Ask what the user wants for self, other, relationship, and long-term outcome.
   - Reject false choices such as honesty versus kindness; search for "and" goals.

3. Watch for safety.
   - Silence signs: withdrawing, avoiding, masking.
   - Violence signs: controlling, labeling, attacking.
   - If safety is low, do not jump to content.

4. Make it safe.
   - Mutual purpose: name the shared outcome.
   - Mutual respect: explicitly preserve dignity.
   - Apologize if the user's behavior caused harm.
   - Contrast: clarify what you do and do not intend.
   - CRIB for purpose gaps: commit to seek mutual purpose, recognize the purpose behind the strategy, invent a mutual purpose, brainstorm strategies.

5. Separate facts from stories.
   - Facts: specific observable behavior.
   - Story: interpretation or conclusion.
   - Feeling: emotional result.
   - Replace accusations with observable facts and tentative interpretations.

6. STATE your path.
   - Share facts.
   - Tell your story.
   - Ask for others' paths.
   - Talk tentatively.
   - Encourage testing.

7. Explore their path with AMPP.
   - Ask, mirror, paraphrase, prime.
   - Keep listening until their facts, story, and feelings are visible.

8. Move to action.
   - Decide how decisions will be made.
   - Record who does what by when, with what follow-up.
   - Confirm unresolved issues and next checkpoint.

## B - Boundary

- If there is immediate danger, abuse, coercion, stalking, threats, or severe harassment, prioritize safety and appropriate professional/legal/HR support.
- Do not help manipulate, trap, or shame the other person.
- Do not script certainty where facts are missing; keep stories tentative.
- Do not treat dialogue as a substitute for enforceable boundaries or formal decisions.
- If the other person refuses dialogue, focus on the user's choices, boundaries, and escalation path.
